A tankless water heater heats water as it flows and has no storage tank, which saves floor space and keeps hot water coming for as long as the tap is open. This page covers tankless water heater installation, repair and descale flush in Wake Forest, NC.

Units do not usually fail in a dramatic way. They throw an error code, run lukewarm or short-cycle. Call the 24/7 line if you have no hot water at all, or book a visit if you are planning an upgrade or a yearly flush.

Is a tankless heater right for your house?

Tankless units suit homes that want continuous hot water, have limited space or want to lower standby heat loss. They do not suit every house. A gas tankless unit needs a gas line large enough to feed it and a vent to the outside. An electric unit needs a large dedicated circuit, and that can mean a panel upgrade.

A plumber surveys the gas line size, vent path, electrical capacity and the number of fixtures you run at once. If those do not line up, a standard tank through water heater installation may be the better fit.

Descale flush and routine care

Raleigh Water's 2025 report lists the water as soft, with about 1.44 to 1.49 grains per gallon, so scale builds more slowly here than in hard-water areas. Even so, makers specify a periodic flush, and a unit that is never serviced eventually loses heat transfer and runs hotter than it should.

A flush uses a small pump to circulate a descaling solution through the heat exchanger via service valves on the unit. If your unit lacks service valves, we install them. We follow the maker's interval, and an inlet filter check goes with each flush.

Common tankless problems

Most service calls fall into a small set of faults.

Error codes

Codes point at ignition, flame, venting or a sensor. The manual's code list narrows the cause.

Lukewarm water

A scaled heat exchanger, a clogged filter or a flow too low to trigger the unit.

Cold water sandwich

A brief burst of cold between two hot flows when the unit is switched on and off in quick succession.

No ignition

A gas supply problem, failed igniter, blocked vent or flame sensor fault.

Short cycling

Often a flow, venting or sizing problem.

Leaking at fittings

Loose or worn connections and valves.

Installation and sizing

Sizing starts with flow rate and temperature rise. Incoming water here is cooler in winter, so the unit has to lift the temperature more, and the output in gallons per minute falls. A plumber works out peak demand from your fixtures and picks a unit that meets it with a margin.

Existing piping is checked for size and condition, and a relief valve and discharge, shutoff valves and any needed condensate drain are included. Questions about tankless water heaters in Wake Forest

How often should a tankless water heater be flushed?

Follow the maker's instructions. Many recommend a flush every year, and some suggest longer intervals in soft-water areas. A plumber can check the heat exchanger and filter during the visit.

Do tankless heaters really provide endless hot water?

They provide hot water for as long as the flow stays within the unit's capacity. Running several showers and a dishwasher at once can exceed it.

Can I convert from a tank to a tankless heater?

Often yes, but the gas line, vent and electrical supply have to be checked. A plumber surveys the house and tells you what the conversion requires.
