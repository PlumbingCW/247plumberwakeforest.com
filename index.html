When a plumbing problem cannot wait

Plumbing emergencies in Wake Forest do not keep office hours. A split supply line floods a kitchen in minutes, a sewer line can back up into a tub on a Sunday night, and a tank that gives out in January leaves a house without hot water when the temperature is lowest. Our emergency plumber crews take calls on the 24/7 line for exactly these situations.

Most of what we see falls into a few groups. A burst pipe or an active leak needs the water stopped first. A blocked fixture or main line needs drain cleaning, and a repeat backup often points to a damaged sewer line. A failed tank calls for water heater repair or a swap through water heater replacement.

Every call starts the same way. You describe what you see, the dispatcher asks a few questions about where the water is and whether it can be shut off, and a plumber is scheduled for the first opening. You hear a clear price before the work begins.

Not every plumbing problem needs a 3 a.m. call. The table below sorts common situations by urgency so you can decide quickly. When in doubt, call. The dispatcher can tell you whether it can wait for morning.

Local note

Plumbing in Wake Forest: water, houses and flood-prone ground

Raleigh Water supplies and bills Wake Forest water and sewer accounts, and its 2025 water quality report lists the water as soft, at about 1.44 to 1.49 grains per gallon. Soft water leaves less scale behind than hard water does, which is one reason tank age and the anode rod are the first things we check on a failing heater.

The town's older housing sits mostly around the historic core. The Glen Royall Mill Village district dates from 1900 to 1949, and the Downtown Wake Forest Historic District covers buildings from about 1890 to 1949. Houses of that age often have older supply and drain lines, so a repair there can turn into a short conversation about repiping or a camera look through sewer camera inspection.

Water also gets into places it should not. The Town's storm notices list flood-prone spots such as Durham Road near Richland Creek and Forestville Road near Toms Creek. If you live near a creek or a low spot, a working sump pump matters before the next heavy rain, not after.

Homes, shops and the Capital Blvd corridor

Capital Blvd (US 1) and NC 98 carry the town's main commercial traffic, and the shops and offices along them need plumbing that works on Monday morning. Our commercial plumbing crews handle after-hours repairs for restaurants, retail units and small offices alongside the residential calls.

At home, the work is just as varied. We install and repair fixtures with plumbing installation, fit new garbage disposals, and fix the toilets and faucets that wear out first. If you want a planned visit rather than an emergency one, say so when you call and we will book a day.

Emergency plumber questions in Wake Forest

Is your emergency plumber really available 24/7 in Wake Forest?

Yes. The dispatch line at (919) 944-7281 is open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, including nights, weekends and holidays. Tell the dispatcher what is happening and your Wake Forest address, and they will book the next available plumber.

We do not promise an arrival window over the phone, because traffic on Capital Blvd, weather and call volume change the answer hour by hour. You will get a straight answer on timing when you call.

What should I do before the plumber gets to my house?

Shut off the main water valve if water is moving where it should not. Turn off the water heater's power or gas supply if the tank is leaking, and stay out of any room where water has reached an outlet or the electrical panel.

If a drain is backing up, stop running water in the house. Every flush or load of laundry pushes more water toward the blockage. The burst pipe repair page and the drain cleaning page list the exact steps for each situation.

How do I check that a plumber is licensed in North Carolina?

The North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors runs a public license search you can use on any plumber before work starts. Our crews are licensed and insured, and you are welcome to verify any contractor you hire, including us.

Under G.S. 87-21, plumbing work done for pay generally requires a license, with exceptions such as cleaning drains and making minor repairs.

Who do I call for a water main problem, you or Raleigh Water?

Raleigh Water operates the water and sewer system serving Wake Forest, and the Town's website says the city repairs damage in the main and between the street main and the meter. Pipes between the meter and your house, or inside it, are the customer's responsibility.

So a gushing main in the street is a call to Raleigh Water at 919-996-3245. A broken pipe in your yard or crawl space is a call to us. If you are unsure, call us and we will tell you which side of the meter the problem is on.

Do I need a permit for a water heater replacement in Wake Forest?

Wake Forest runs its own Inspections Department at 301 S. Brooks St., and the Town's pages do not spell out the water heater rule, so we tell homeowners to call 919-435-9531 before any change. Wake County's own guidance exempts like-for-like replacement in a one or two family home when the fuel, location and venting stay the same and a licensed plumber does the work, but that guidance covers the towns Wake County permits for.

Our crews will tell you when a change-out needs a permit. See water heater replacement for how we handle it.

How do you price an emergency call?

You get a clear price before work starts, and you decide whether to go ahead. Cost depends on what is failing, how hard it is to reach and whether parts are on the truck. The cost sections on each service page, such as sewer line repair, list the factors in plain terms.

Insurance can matter too. Many homeowner policies cover sudden pipe bursts but not wear or neglected maintenance, so ask your insurer before you need to.
