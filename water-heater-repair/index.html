Water heater trouble gets urgent fast. A tank that is leaking can flood a utility closet or garage, and a heater that has quit leaves a household without hot water when it is coldest. This page covers emergency water heater repair in Wake Forest, NC, and the 24/7 line is open now.

While you wait, turn off the power at the breaker for an electric heater or turn the gas control to off for a gas one. If water is pooling, close the cold-water shutoff valve on top of the tank. Do not touch the unit if the floor around it is wet and an outlet is nearby.

Leaking tank or no hot water: which one are you dealing with?

These are two different jobs. No hot water usually means a part has failed, such as a heating element, a thermostat, a pilot assembly or a gas valve. Those are repairs. A tank that is leaking from the bottom or the seams is different, because the steel has failed from the inside and no repair holds.

Water at a fitting on top or at the relief valve is often fixable. Water coming out of the tank body is not. A plumber can tell the two apart in minutes. If the tank is the source, the honest answer is a swap through water heater replacement, and we say so before you pay for a repair that will not last.

Gas, electric and hybrid units

Gas heaters have a burner, a pilot or igniter, a flue and a gas control valve, and a repair that touches gas gets a leak check and a venting check before we leave. Electric heaters have two elements and thermostats, and a failed element is the most common repair on that type. Hybrid heat pump heaters add a compressor and fan, and some faults need the maker's service channel.

Tankless units fail differently, usually through scale, a blocked inlet filter or a sensor fault, and have their own page at tankless water heater.

Why Wake Forest water is easier on tanks, and what still wears them out

Raleigh Water's 2025 report lists the water supplied to Wake Forest as soft, at about 1.44 to 1.49 grains per gallon, with a pH near 8.4. Soft water drops less scale on elements and the tank floor than hard water does, so we look at age and the anode rod first.

The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes so the steel tank does not. Once it is used up, the tank starts to corrode. Many tanks reach the end of their life somewhere in a range of roughly 8 to 12 years, and a heater past ten that develops a leak or a second failure is usually cheaper to replace than to keep repairing.

What a repair visit looks like

The visit follows the same order every time so nothing is missed.

  1. Check the safety basics

    The plumber confirms the power or gas is off, the relief valve is intact and nothing is leaking from the tank body.

  2. Find the failed part

    Meters and gauges confirm which element, thermostat or valve is at fault rather than guessing.

  3. Quote, then repair

    You hear the price for the repair and, if relevant, what a replacement would cost instead.

  4. Test and flush if needed

    The heater is tested for temperature and relief valve operation, and sediment is flushed when it is the cause.

Questions about water heater repair in Wake Forest

Do you handle water heater emergencies at night or on weekends in Wake Forest?

Yes. Call the 24/7 line at (919) 944-7281 and tell the dispatcher whether the heater is leaking or has stopped heating. A plumber is scheduled for the next opening, and you hear the price for repair before the work starts.

A leaking tank is treated as more urgent than a heater that has simply gone cold, but both are same-day calls when a plumber is open.

Why does my water heater have hot water in the morning but run out quickly?

A failed lower element or a broken dip tube are the common causes. A lower element failure leaves you only the top third of the tank, and a dip tube failure mixes cold water into the hot outlet. Both are repairs.

Is it safe to keep using a leaking water heater?

No. Shut off power or gas and close the cold-water valve above the tank. A leak can worsen without warning and cause water damage or an electrical hazard if the floor is wet.

How long should a tank water heater last?

Many tanks last roughly 8 to 12 years depending on use, water quality and maintenance. If yours is near the top of that range and starts to leak, replacement is usually the better value.
