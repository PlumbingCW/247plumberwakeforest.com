How to Replace a Toilet Wax Ring: Wake Forest, NC Owner Guide

A failed wax ring leaks at the base of the toilet and can rot the floor. Replacing one is a manageable job if you know the steps and the limits.

Call (919) 944-7281

By the Wake Forest 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026

A wax ring is a soft seal between the base of a toilet and the drain flange in the floor. It stops water and sewer gas from escaping. When it fails, you see water or staining around the toilet base, smell sewage or feel the toilet rock.

Replacing it takes about an hour for a careful homeowner, but it is messy, and some situations are better left to a plumber.

Signs the wax ring has failed

These are the typical signs.

Water at the base after a flush

The seal no longer contains the discharge.

A toilet that rocks

A loose toilet breaks the seal over time.

Sewer odor in the bathroom

Gas escapes around a failed seal.

Stained or soft floor near the toilet

Slow leaks soak the subfloor.

A recent toilet move or flange repair

A new setup needs a new seal.

What you need before you start

You need a new wax ring, or a wax-free rubber seal if you prefer, new closet bolts, a putty knife, a wrench, rags, a bucket and a helper. A toilet is heavy and awkward. Close the supply valve, flush to empty the tank and sponge out what remains in the bowl and tank.

The steps, in order

Work slowly and keep the toilet upright when you lift it.

  1. Shut off and drain

    Close the supply valve, flush and sponge out the tank and bowl. Disconnect the supply line.

  2. Remove the toilet

    Take off the nuts on the closet bolts, rock the toilet gently to break the seal and lift it straight up. Set it on a towel or cardboard.

  3. Clean the flange

    Scrape off the old wax, check the flange for cracks and confirm it sits at or just above the finished floor.

  4. Set the new ring

    Place the new ring on the flange or on the toilet horn, and fit new closet bolts.

  5. Lower the toilet

    Line it up with the bolts and press it straight down. Do not twist.

  6. Tighten and test

    Tighten the nuts evenly, reconnect the supply, flush several times and check for leaks.

Wax ring or wax-free seal?

A standard wax ring is inexpensive and seals well on a level flange. A thicker or extra-wide ring handles a flange that sits slightly low. Wax-free seals use rubber or foam and tolerate small movements. They can be repositioned, which helps if you set the toilet crooked the first time.

Either works when installed correctly. The choice matters less than a solid flange and a toilet that sits firmly. A plumber carries both and picks the one that suits your flange.

Why toilets lose their seal

Toilets lose their seal when they move. A floor that flexes, bolts that loosened over time or a flange that sits below a new tile floor are common reasons. A toilet that has been rocked for months wears its ring flat. A ring that was compressed once and reused never seals as well the second time.

Leaking base or leaking tank?

Condensation on a cold tank, loose tank bolts and a worn supply line connection can all drip onto the floor and look like a base leak. Dry the area, put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait. If the color shows up at the base without a flush, the tank is leaking. If water appears only after a flush, the base seal has failed.

When to call a plumber

Call if the flange is damaged, the floor is soft, the toilet is wall-hung or you are unsure how to lift the weight safely. A plumber also checks for the underlying cause of the leak. Our toilet repair page lists the other common toilet faults. If the toilet drains slowly instead of leaking, the problem may sit in the line, which is a job for drain cleaning.

Prevent a repeat

Do not caulk the base all the way around, because a hidden leak then has no path to show itself. Do not use a wax ring that has been compressed once. Questions about toilet repair in Wake Forest

Do I need a permit to reset a toilet in Wake Forest?

A simple reset with a new wax ring is a minor repair. Wake Forest runs its own Inspections Department at 919-435-9531, and a call confirms what applies if you also move piping.

Should I use a wax ring or a rubber seal?

Both work. Wax rings are common and inexpensive. Rubber or foam seals are reusable and tolerant of a slightly uneven flange. A plumber picks based on the flange height.
