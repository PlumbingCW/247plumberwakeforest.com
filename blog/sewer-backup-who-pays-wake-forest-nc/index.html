Sewer Backup in Wake Forest, NC: Who Pays, You or Raleigh?

When sewage backs up, the first question is whose pipe it is. Raleigh Water's own guidance gives a clear answer for most homes.

Call (919) 944-7281

By the Wake Forest 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026

A sewer backup is stressful, and the first question is usually who pays. In Wake Forest the answer comes from Raleigh Water, which runs the sewer system that serves the town. Its published guidance explains where the city's responsibility ends and the homeowner's begins.

This post explains that guidance in plain terms, what to do in the first hour and how to talk to the city.

What to do in the first hour

These steps limit damage and protect you if you need to file a claim.

  1. Stop all water use

    Do not flush, run the dishwasher or the washing machine. Each use pushes more water toward the blockage.

  2. Keep people and pets out

    Sewage carries bacteria. Stay out of affected rooms and wear gloves and boots if you must enter.

  3. Call Raleigh Water

    The city's sewer FAQ lists 919-996-3245 for reporting a backup.

  4. Call a plumber

    A plumber clears the line and checks whether the problem is private or in the street.

  5. Document everything

    Take photographs and video, keep receipts and write down times and names.

What the city says about ownership

Raleigh Water's homeowner guidance says the homeowner maintains the sanitary sewer service from the house to the city's sewer, which sits in the street or an easement. It says the entire service belongs to the homeowner and should be cleaned in its entirety. It says a sanitary sewer cleanout should be at the property line, and that a missing cleanout has to be installed.

The same guidance says that if a camera inspection finds damage in the street portion, the homeowner should contact Raleigh Public Utilities. City staff will visit and verify with your plumber whether repairs are needed. It says fewer than 1.0% of service backups are found to be the city's responsibility.

Local note

Wake Forest local note: where the city's role is clear

The Town of Wake Forest's website says Raleigh Water took sole responsibility for operating, maintaining and expanding the system serving Wake Forest, and that it bills all water and sewer accounts. Raleigh Water lists 919-996-3245 as the number for questions about water and sewer service, and for reporting a backup.

For you, that means one phone number for the city side and one for the plumbing side. Call both. The city can confirm whether the main is blocked, and your plumber can clear and camera the private line. Keep the video, because it is the proof the city asks for. Source type: Town of Wake Forest utilities page and Raleigh Water sewer FAQ.

Asking your plumber the right questions

The city's guidance says that if a plumber clears a blockage, you should ask them to clear it all the way to the city main. It also says a plumber who says they cannot clean the street portion is incorrect, because the whole service belongs to the homeowner. A good plumber agrees to both.

Ask for a camera inspection after the line is cleared. The recording shows whether the pipe is sound, and it supports any conversation with the city. Our sewer line cleaning page explains how we clear a main line, and a sewer camera inspection shows what is inside.

Why private sewer lines back up

Most backups come from a blockage in the line between the house and the main. Grease that cools and hardens, wipes that do not break down and roots that enter through small cracks are the usual causes. Raleigh Water's fats, oils and grease program tells residents not to pour grease down the drain.

Older clay or cast iron lines also shift, sag or crack with age. A camera inspection shows which problem you have, and the answer decides whether a cleaning is enough or the line needs repair.

Cleanouts and why they help

A cleanout is a capped access point on the sewer line. It lets a plumber send a cable or a camera into the line without pulling a toilet. Raleigh Water's guidance says a cleanout should sit at the property line and that a missing one needs to be installed.

If your house has no cleanout, ask your plumber to add one the next time the line is open. It makes every later cleaning faster and easier to document.

Insurance and cleanup

Homeowner insurance may cover damage from a sewer backup only with an added endorsement. Call your agent before cleanup begins, and ask what documentation is needed. Cleanup of sewage-soaked materials is a restoration job, not plumbing. Keep a record of anything removed.

Prevent the next backup

Keep grease, wipes, diapers and floss out of the drain. Raleigh Water's sewer FAQ lists them among the things not to flush or pour. Install a cleanout at the property line if you lack one. If the line is old or roots are an issue, a camera inspection and a repair through sewer line repair is the lasting fix.

Questions about sewer backups in Wake Forest

Will Raleigh Water pay for my sewer backup damage?

The city's guidance says the service from the house to the main belongs to the homeowner and that fewer than 1.0% of backups are the city's responsibility. If a camera finds damage in the street portion, city staff will verify with your plumber. Call 919-996-3245.

Should I call the city before a plumber?

Call both. The city's sewer FAQ says to call 919-996-3245 to report a backup, and it does not say to wait for the city before hiring a plumber.
