Faucet Handle Repair in Wake Forest, NC: Fix or Replace?

A handle that wobbles, sticks or drips at the base is usually a small part. Here is how to tell which, and when a new faucet is the better call.

Call (919) 944-7281

By the Wake Forest 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026

A faucet handle problem feels bigger than it is. Most of the time a screw has loosened, a cartridge has worn or a seal has hardened. Fixing it takes a few tools and a bit of patience.

This guide walks through the common causes and helps you decide whether to repair or replace.

Match the symptom to the cause

Faucet handle problems and fixes

Handle is stiffWorn or scaled cartridgeReplace the cartridge

Steps for a loose or leaking handle

These steps apply to most single-handle faucets. Two-handle faucets follow the same pattern for each handle.

  1. Turn off the water

    Close the shutoff valves under the sink, then open the faucet to relieve pressure.

  2. Remove the handle

    Look for a small cap or a set screw. Pry off the cap and loosen the screw with a hex key.

  3. Inspect the cartridge

    Pull the retaining clip or nut and lift the cartridge. Look for wear, scale or cracked seals.

  4. Replace the part

    Take the old cartridge to a supplier to match the model, then install the new one in the same orientation.

  5. Reassemble and test

    Replace the handle, open the shutoff valves and run the faucet. Check for drips.

Local note

Wake Forest local note: soft water and the bill

Raleigh Water's 2025 report lists the water supplied to Wake Forest as soft, at about 1.44 to 1.49 grains per gallon. Soft water leaves fewer mineral deposits inside cartridges and aerators than hard water does, so a stiff handle in Wake Forest is more often a worn part than a scaled one.

Raleigh Water also states that its leak adjustment treats a dripping faucet as an accessible leak. For those, water charges are adjusted to double the monthly average, but wastewater charges are not.

That adjustment needs usage at least twice your monthly average and proof of repair, so a slow drip will not qualify. Still, keep the receipt if a faucet ran for weeks. Source type: Raleigh Water report and leak adjustment page.

Handle styles and what each one hides

A lever handle on a kitchen faucet often hides a ball or cartridge under a small cap. A knob handle on a bathroom faucet usually hides a ceramic disc cartridge or a compression stem. A cross handle on an older two-handle faucet sits on a stem with a rubber washer. Each style wears in its own way.

Before you begin, take a photo of the handle and the base from a few angles. Keep removed parts in order on a towel. A set of hex keys in both metric and inch sizes saves a trip to the store.

Mistakes that turn a small repair into a bigger one

The most common mistake is to force a stuck handle with pliers, which scratches the finish and can crack a plastic handle. Another is to over-tighten the set screw and strip it. A third is to leave the sink drain open while working, and a dropped screw disappears for good. Close the stopper or place a rag in the drain first.

When replacing the faucet is smarter

Replace the faucet when the body is corroded, parts are no longer made or the finish is failing. A fifteen or twenty year old faucet with a leaking handle often has several worn parts. The cost of a cartridge plus time may approach the cost of a new faucet.

A new faucet also lets you upgrade to a model with a lower flow rate. Installing one is covered under plumbing installation, and the repair itself falls under faucet repair.

Keeping a faucet working longer

A few habits stretch the life of a faucet. Close handles gently rather than cranking them. Clean aerators when flow drops. Replace a cartridge when the handle first feels rough, since a worn cartridge scores the body and shortens the next one's life. If you hear water hammer when a handle closes, mention it to a plumber, because pressure spikes wear parts quickly.

When to stop and call

Call if the shutoff valves under the sink will not close, if water appears behind a wall or under a vanity, or if the handle is part of a shower valve in a finished wall. A shower valve repair may require removing tile or opening the wall behind it, and a plumber does that with less damage.

Questions about faucet repair in Wake Forest

Will a dripping faucet reduce my Raleigh Water bill if I fix it?

A drip rarely qualifies for a leak adjustment, because usage must be at least twice your monthly average. Fixing it still stops the waste. Raleigh Water asks for a plumber's receipt when you do apply.

Can I use a universal cartridge to fix my faucet?

Sometimes, but cartridges are brand and model specific. A cartridge that is close but not exact can leak or limit the handle's travel. Bring the old cartridge to the supplier and match it by sight.

Why does my handle feel stiff even though the water is soft?

In soft water, stiffness is more often a worn cartridge or a dried seal than scale. Replacing the cartridge is the usual fix.
