Plumbing a New Bathroom in Wake Forest, NC: Homeowner Guide

A new bathroom is mostly a drain and vent problem, with supply lines as the easier half. Plan those first and the rest follows.

Call (919) 944-7281

By the Wake Forest 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026

Adding a bathroom changes the plumbing of the whole house. It needs new supply lines for hot and cold water, a drain that slopes to the main stack and a vent so the drain flows freely. Planning those in the right order saves money and time.

This guide walks through the sequence, the decisions to make first and the permit questions to settle before work begins.

Decide the location first

The location drives the cost. A bathroom stacked above or beside an existing one can share the drain and vent. A bathroom on the opposite side of the house needs a new run, and on a slab that means cutting concrete. Walk the space with a plumber before you finalize a layout.

Ask where the existing stack, the main drain and the supply lines are. A short walk through the crawl space or attic tells a plumber most of what is needed.

The fixtures list

Make the fixture list before the rough-in. A toilet needs a flange and a drain, usually three or four inches in diameter. A sink needs a smaller drain and a trap. A tub or shower needs a drain, a valve and a spout or showerhead. A heated floor, a bidet or a double vanity changes the plan.

Standard toilet rough-in is twelve inches from the wall to the center of the drain, but older houses may use ten or fourteen. Pick fixtures before the rough-in so the pipes land where the fixtures need them.

Local note

Wake Forest local note: which permit and who to call

Wake County's guidance says a plumbing permit is required for the installation, extension, relocation, replacement or general repair of any plumbing system. It says a trade permit is for work that does not need a building permit, and that remodeling work such as opening walls or changing framing needs a Residential Building Alteration Permit instead. It lists a $75 fee for a residential trade permit, covering electrical, mechanical and plumbing.

That is the County's rule for the towns it permits, such as Rolesville. Wake Forest has its own Inspections Department at 301 S. Brooks St., phone 919-435-9531, and the Town's pages do not list a fee. A new bathroom opens walls and changes framing, so expect a building permit as well as a plumbing permit, and confirm both with the office. Source type: Wake County and Town of Wake Forest permit pages.

Venting, slope and the details inspectors check

Drains need a steady slope so water carries waste along. A vent lets air into the system so water does not pull the trap dry. Both are among the first things an inspector checks. A bathroom that is vented poorly gurgles, drains slowly and can smell. The fix after walls are closed is expensive.

Supply lines are more forgiving. They are pressure tested before walls close, and a leak shows up on the gauge. Shutoff valves for each fixture are standard and worth having.

Water heater capacity and pressure

A new bathroom adds demand on the water heater. A tank that served two baths may struggle with three. Ask your plumber whether the heater can keep up or whether a larger tank or a tankless unit is worth considering. See water heater installation for the options.

Check house pressure too. A new shower on a weak supply line is a disappointment. A plumber can test pressure and flow before you commit to fixtures.

Surprises that change the budget

The usual surprises are hidden. A drain that is not where the drawings said, a floor joist in the path of a new line or an old galvanized supply that cannot be tied into without replacement. Each adds time and cost, and a plumber who has seen the space can flag the likely ones early.

Another surprise is the finish work. Tile, vanities and glass are chosen late, but their sizes decide where valves and drains land. Settle those choices before the rough-in so the pipes are not moved after the walls are closed.

Water supply and shutoffs

Every new fixture should have its own shutoff valve. That lets you repair a toilet or a sink without turning off the whole house. Ask for quarter-turn valves, which are easier to use and less likely to seize than older multi-turn types.

Think about where the shutoff for the whole bathroom group will sit too. A valve at the branch lets a plumber isolate the room during a later repair.

Questions about adding a bathroom in Wake Forest

Which office handles permits for a new bathroom in Wake Forest?

The Town of Wake Forest Inspections Department at 919-435-9531 handles permits and inspections inside the Town. Wake County's guidance covers the towns it permits and calls for a Residential Building Alteration Permit when walls are opened.

Can I add a bathroom on a slab foundation?

Yes, but the drain usually means cutting the slab and trenching to the main line. That raises the cost, so the layout matters.
