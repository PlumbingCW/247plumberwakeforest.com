Garbage Disposal Cost in Wake Forest, NC: What Sets the Price

A disposal price is built from the unit, the labor and the plumbing around it. Here is what changes the number and how to avoid paying for a clog you caused.

Call (919) 944-7281

By the Wake Forest 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026

Homeowners searching for the cost of a garbage disposal usually want one number. A single figure would mislead you, because the price depends on the unit you choose, what is already under your sink and whether the job is a swap or a first installation. This guide explains how the price is built so you can read a quote with confidence.

If your current disposal is jammed or leaking, our garbage disposal repair page covers what to do tonight. This post is for planning a purchase.

The three parts of a disposal quote

Every quote has the same three pieces. The first is the unit itself, and units are sold by motor power, from about one half horsepower to a full horsepower. The second is labor, which covers removing the old unit, mounting the new one and connecting the drain, the dishwasher line and the power. The third is anything the job turns up, such as a corroded drain fitting or a missing outlet.

A plug-in replacement of a similar size under an existing sink is the simplest case and the least costly. A first installation where no disposal existed adds an electrical connection, a switch and often a new drain arrangement, so it costs more.

What a bigger motor does and does not buy you

A larger motor handles tougher scraps and tends to jam less often. It does not make the disposal safe for grease, bones or stringy vegetables. A small family that cooks lightly does well with a half to three quarter horsepower unit. A household that cooks every night may justify a bigger one.

Sound insulation is the other upgrade people notice. If the kitchen is open to the living room, quieter operation is worth a little more.

Local note

Wake Forest local note: Raleigh Water's grease rules

Raleigh Water runs the sewer system for Wake Forest, and its Fats, Oils and Grease Program tells residents that even where a grease trap is present, disposals are not designed to handle fats, oils and grease. It recommends wiping grease from pans and plates with a paper towel and throwing it in the trash. It also warns that sponges and mops rinsed in the sink send grease into the sewer.

The practical effect for a buyer is simple. A new disposal will not stop a grease clog, and a more powerful one costs more without changing that. If your sink slows down every few months, the cheaper fix is a habit change plus a drain cleaning visit, not a bigger motor. Source type: Raleigh Water program page.

When replacement beats repair

A disposal that leaks from the bottom or the side of the housing has failed internally and cannot be sealed. A jam, a tripped reset or a loose connection is a repair. Age matters too. Disposals commonly last somewhere around 8 to 12 years, and a unit near the end of that range with a second fault is usually better replaced.

Replacement is also the moment to fix what the old unit hid. A cracked drain elbow, a worn sink flange or a stained cabinet floor is cheaper to handle while the sink is open.

Questions to ask before you accept a quote

A clear quote answers these without being asked.

Is the unit included?

Some quotes cover labor only and leave you to buy the disposal.

Is the electrical connection included?

A plug-in swap is simple. A hardwired switch or a new outlet adds work.

Who removes the old unit?

Haul-away is routine but should be stated.

What happens if the line is clogged?

A clog behind the disposal is a separate drain cleaning job.

Is the dishwasher drain reconnected?

A missed knockout plug means the dishwasher will not drain.

What warranty applies?

Makers warrant parts, and the installer warrants labor. Ask for both in writing.

Costs that surprise people

The unit price is rarely the surprise. The surprises sit under the sink. A drain elbow that has corroded, a sink flange that no longer seats, or a dishwasher line that was never fitted with an air gap can each add work. So can a missing outlet, because a plug-in disposal needs power within reach and a switch you can use safely.

Cabinet space matters too. Some units are taller or wider than the one they replace, and a crowded cabinet can force a different model. Measure the space and note any pipes or filters that share it. If you plan to add a water filter or an instant hot tap, say so, because they compete for the same room.

Questions about disposal costs in Wake Forest

Does Raleigh Water say anything about garbage disposals?

Yes. Its Fats, Oils and Grease Program says disposals are not designed to handle grease, even where a grease trap is present. It asks residents to wipe pans, collect cooled grease and throw it away.

Does a disposal increase my water bill?

Only slightly. A disposal uses a small amount of water while it runs, and running it for a short time with the cold tap open is enough. A leak under the sink is a bigger bill risk than the disposal itself.

Is it cheaper to install a disposal myself?

The unit costs the same either way, but a mistake can mean a leak under the sink or a dishwasher that will not drain. A plumber also handles the electrical connection and tests the drain.
