Can You Repipe Your Own House? Wake Forest, NC Rules and Risks

Repiping is one of the largest plumbing jobs in a home. Before you pick up a PEX crimper, read what the law says and what can go wrong.

Call (919) 944-7281

By the Wake Forest 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026

Repiping replaces the water supply lines in a house. It is a big job, with long runs, many fittings and a lot of walls, ceilings and floors in the way. Some owners ask whether they can do it themselves to save money. The answer depends on the law, the permit and your own skill.

This guide explains the rules that apply in Wake Forest and the risks that often catch DIY repipes.

What North Carolina's licensing law says

North Carolina's plumbing licensing law is Chapter 87, Article 2 of the General Statutes. Section 87-21 applies to people who do plumbing work for a valuable consideration, meaning for pay. It also treats a person who installs a system on property intended for sale or used primarily for rental as a contractor.

It exempts those who clean plumbing drains or make minor repairs. It defines minor repairs as replacing parts that do not change the energy source, fuel type or the routing or sizing of venting or piping.

In plain terms, a repipe is not a minor repair, and whether you need a license turns on whether the work is paid and what the property is. Your permit obligations are a separate question.

How to check a contractor's license

The North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors runs a public license search. If you hire a plumber for a repipe, look up the license before work starts and ask to see proof of insurance. The same search works on us, and we welcome it.

Local note

Wake Forest local note: permits and inspections

Wake County's permit guidance says a plumbing permit is required for the installation, extension, relocation, replacement or general repair of any plumbing system, and it says this covers repiping. That guidance applies to the towns Wake County permits for. The Town of Wake Forest has its own Inspections Department at 301 S. Brooks St., phone 919-435-9531, which provides building inspections and permitting inside the Town.

If you plan any repipe in Wake Forest, call the Inspections Department first. Ask what permit applies, what inspections are required and who is allowed to pull the permit. Source type: Wake County and Town of Wake Forest permit pages.

Where DIY repipes go wrong

These are the failure points that cost owners the most.

Poor connections

A single bad fitting can leak inside a wall for weeks before it shows.

Wrong pipe supports

Unsupported runs sag, rattle and loosen fittings over time.

No pressure test

A test before the walls are closed catches leaks that are cheap to fix at that point.

Unpermitted work

It can cause problems at resale and with insurance claims.

Mixed materials

Joining dissimilar metals without the right fittings invites corrosion.

Underestimating the time

A house repipe is days of work with water off for long stretches.

What a full repipe involves day to day

A whole-house repipe usually starts with a plan for where the new lines will run and where walls or ceilings need small access holes. Water is shut off while old lines are cut out and new ones are connected, so the household has to plan for stretches without running water.

After the new lines are in, the system is pressure tested before any patching begins. Drywall repair comes last, and it is often a separate trade. Ask a plumber who handles the patching and how many days the house will be without water.

Partial repipes and staged work

Not every house needs every line replaced. A single failing branch, such as the kitchen or a bathroom group, can be replaced on its own. A staged repipe spreads the work across several visits, which helps when walls are finished and budgets are tight.

The tradeoff is that a staged job can leave old and new materials side by side. Ask how the joints between old and new pipe will be made and which fittings are listed for both materials. A plumber can tell you whether staging saves money or only delays it.

A middle path

Some owners do the prep and leave the plumbing to a licensed contractor. Clear access to crawl spaces and attics, move furniture and photograph the existing lines. That cuts labor time and cost without putting your house at risk. A plumber can also do a partial repipe, replacing only the worst section.

See our repiping page for how a plumber approaches the job and what drives the cost. If you are not sure the pipes are the problem, a leak detection visit shows where the water is going.

What to do next

Decide whether the pipes really need replacing. Signs include repeated pinhole leaks, rusty water and low pressure. Then call the Inspections Department about permits and a licensed plumber about a quote. We serve Wake Forest and Rolesville through our Rolesville page, and the full area is on the service area page.

Do not guess about your own insurance either. Some policies exclude damage from unpermitted work, and a leak in a self-installed system is the owner's cost. A call to your agent before you start is cheap.

Questions about DIY repiping in Wake Forest

Who do I call about a repipe permit in Wake Forest?

The Town of Wake Forest Inspections Department at 919-435-9531, at 301 S. Brooks St. Wake County's guidance says a plumbing permit is required for replacement of any plumbing system, including repiping, but Wake Forest has its own office.

How can I check whether a plumber is licensed in North Carolina?

Use the public license search run by the State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors at public.nclicensing.org. Look up the name and license before work starts.
