Plumbing installation covers anything new: a bathroom added to a house, a kitchen remodel, a laundry room moved, a new sink or tub, or a new shutoff where none existed. This page covers plumbing installation in Wake Forest, NC.

Installation is planned work, so you can book a day. If something is leaking right now, the 24/7 line is open and the repair pages cover it. If you want to add or move plumbing, this page explains how projects are scoped.

What we install

The common projects are a new or remodeled bathroom, a kitchen sink and dishwasher hookup, a utility sink, a laundry box and drain, outdoor hose bibs and a new water heater. We also install toilets, faucets, showers and tubs, and relocate lines for remodels.

A fixture swap in the same spot is the simplest. A fixture that moves needs new supply and drain lines, a vent and sometimes a new route through the floor or wall. That is where planning matters.

Planning a new or remodeled bathroom

A bathroom plan starts with where the drain and vent go. Drains need slope, and the vent needs a path to the roof or an approved connection. Moving a toilet more than a short distance can mean cutting into the floor, and on a slab it means breaking concrete.

Supply lines are easier to route than drains. A plumber walks the space, checks the existing stack and branch lines and builds the plan around them. A short walk through saves cost and surprises.

Permits and who to call

Wake County's permit guidance says a plumbing permit is needed for installation, extension, relocation, replacement or general repair of any plumbing system, while minor fixture repairs that do not disrupt supply, waste or ventilation are exempt. It also says remodeling work that opens walls or changes framing needs a Residential Building Alteration Permit instead of a trade permit.

That guidance covers the towns Wake County permits for. Wake Forest has its own Inspections Department at 301 S. Brooks St., phone 919-435-9531, and the right call before work starts is to that office. A licensed plumber familiar with the process will tell you what your project needs.

Fixtures and the water they use

Choose fixtures with water use in mind. Toilets that meet the EPA's WaterSense standard use 1.28 gallons per flush or less, and WaterSense showerheads and faucets cut flow as well. Raleigh Water bills Wake Forest accounts for water and sewer, so lower use shows up on the bill.

Plan for access, too. A tub or shower valve behind a finished wall is hard to reach later, so we install an access panel where it makes sense.

Which code governs new plumbing

New fixtures, drains and vents in Wake Forest are installed to the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code. Inspectors check slope, venting and supply connections against it, which is why the order of work matters and why walls stay open until the rough-in passes.

Questions about plumbing installation in Wake Forest

Do I need a permit to add a bathroom?

Yes, a new bathroom involves new supply, drain and vent lines and permits apply. Wake Forest has its own Inspections Department at 919-435-9531, and a licensed plumber can pull the permit.

Can I move a toilet across the room?

Often yes, but it depends on the floor and the drain. On a slab, the drain has to be broken out and rerouted. A plumber checks access before quoting.

How long does a bathroom rough-in take?

A rough-in for a new bathroom usually takes a day or two, depending on access. Inspections and finishing take additional time.
