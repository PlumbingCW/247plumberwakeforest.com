Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Wake Forest, NC

The pipe from the meter to your house is yours to maintain. When it leaks or breaks, we find the break and fix or replace it.

Call (919) 944-7281

A water service line carries water from the main in the street, through the meter, to your house. When it breaks, you may see a wet yard, a drop in pressure or a water bill that has climbed. This is emergency water service line repair in Wake Forest, NC, and the 24/7 line is open now.

While you wait, shut off the main valve at the house or the shutoff at the meter box if you can reach it safely. Do not dig near a suspected leak. Buried gas, power and water lines may sit close together, and North Carolina's 811 one-call center marks utilities before any digging.

Who is responsible: Raleigh Water or you?

The Town of Wake Forest's website says Raleigh Water operates the water system, and that the city repairs the main and the pipe between the street main and the meter. It also says that when pipes break between the water meter and the home, or inside the home, the customer is responsible for repairs.

In practice, a leak at the meter is a call to Raleigh Water at 919-996-3245. A leak on your side of the meter, between the meter and the house, is a call to us. The Town's page lists a weekday number, 919-996-4540, and an after-hours number, 919-829-1930, for Raleigh Water, and we suggest confirming both on the Town's site because numbers change.

Signs your service line is failing

Buried leaks hide, but they leave clues.

A meter that moves with taps off

The simplest test. Close every tap and watch the leak indicator.

Wet or soft ground in the yard

A patch that stays green or soggy in dry weather points at a buried leak.

Pressure drops

Low flow at every fixture, not just one, suggests a restriction or leak upstream of the house.

Higher water bills

A rise with no change in use is often a leak. Raleigh Water's leak adjustment may apply.

Sound of running water

A hissing pipe in a quiet house points at a leak on the line or inside.

Discolored water

Rust or sediment after repairs or a break may mean pipe wear.

Repair or replace the line

A single leak on a sound pipe can be repaired by digging to the break and installing a new section. A line that has leaked more than once, or that is made of old galvanized steel, is usually worth replacing. Replacement uses modern copper or plastic pipe rated for underground use and runs from the meter to the house.

A trenchless method pulls the new pipe through the path of the old one, which saves a lawn or driveway when conditions allow. A plumber confirms what fits after locating the existing line. If the leak is under a slab, it is a leak detection and slab repair question rather than a yard dig.

Low pressure, high pressure and the pressure reducing valve

Not every pressure problem is a leak. A pressure reducing valve near the main shutoff can wear out and pass too much or too little pressure. Too much pressure strains fittings and shortens the life of faucets and appliances. Too little can mean a restriction in the line or in the valve.

A gauge on an outdoor hose bib shows the pressure in a few seconds. If the reading is off, the valve or the line is the likely cause, and a plumber can tell which.

Questions about water service lines in Wake Forest

Do you handle water service line emergencies at night or on weekends in Wake Forest?

Yes. Call (919) 944-7281 any hour. A broken service line, a flooded yard or a sudden loss of water belongs on the 24/7 line. Close the main valve if you can and call.

If water is gushing in the street near the meter, call Raleigh Water at 919-996-3245 as well, since the main and the meter connection are theirs.

How do I know if the leak is on my side of the meter?

Close the shutoff valve at the house and watch the meter. If it still moves, the leak is between the meter and the valve, which is on the customer side. If it stops, the leak is inside the house.

Will Raleigh Water reduce my bill after a service line leak?

Raleigh Water's leak adjustment may apply if usage was at least twice your monthly average and the leak was on the customer side of the meter. It requires proof of repair, and the city decides eligibility.

Do I need to call before digging?

Yes. North Carolina's 811 service marks buried utilities before any excavation. A plumber normally handles the call before digging begins.
