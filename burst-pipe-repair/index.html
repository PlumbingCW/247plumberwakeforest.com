Burst pipe: what to do in the first ten minutes

These steps limit damage while you wait for a plumber.

  1. Stop the water

    Close the main shutoff valve. Turn the handle fully. If it will not turn, the shutoff at the meter box can be used and a plumber can assist.

  2. Drain the lines

    Open a faucet at the lowest point of the house and flush upper toilets once to relieve pressure.

  3. Protect the electrics

    If water has reached an outlet, light or the panel, turn off power from the breaker box only if the floor around it is dry. Otherwise call the utility or 911.

  4. Document the damage

    Take photographs and video before you move anything. Insurers ask for them.

  5. Call dispatch

    Tell them where the pipe burst and whether the water is stopped.

Why pipes burst, and where

A supply pipe splits for a handful of reasons. Freezing is the best known, when ice expands inside a pipe and splits it, though the break may only show once the pipe thaws. Corrosion thins old galvanized and aging copper from the inside, and water hammer or high pressure fatigues joints. Poor soldering, shifting soil and a failed fitting behind a wall do the rest.

Wake Forest sits in the Piedmont and does not see the long deep freezes of colder states, but a hard overnight freeze can still crack exposed lines in a crawl space, garage or outside wall. The Town of Wake Forest's own notice to residents says a customer is responsible for pipes that break between the water meter and the home, or inside it.

Frozen pipes and what to do before they split

Frozen pipes are a warning sign, not yet a burst. If a faucet gives only a trickle on a freezing night, leave it open, keep cabinet doors under sinks open and let the house warm. Never use an open flame to thaw a pipe. A hair dryer on low at a safe distance is safer, and turn the water off at the main first.

Once a pipe thaws, check for leaks along its length. Many breaks are not obvious until water flows again. If you find one, the steps above apply and the repair is the same.

How a burst pipe gets repaired

The plumber confirms the water is off, finds the full extent of the damage and decides between a spot repair and replacing a longer run. A pinhole can be sleeved. A long split or heavily corroded pipe is better replaced, because patching a weak pipe tends to move the failure a few feet along.

After the repair the line is pressure tested before any wall is closed. Water damage restoration, drywall and drying are separate trades. If the leak ran for days or sits in a slab, a leak detection visit confirms there are no more weak points.

When a burst pipe means a bigger decision

One burst in a house with older lines can be an isolated failure. Two in a year are usually a pattern. At that point, replacing the line through repiping can cost less than repeated emergency visits, and a plumber can show you how much of the system is at risk.

A burst on the outside line between the street and the house is a water service line job, and a failed drain can look like a burst but is handled as sewer line repair.

Where is my main water shutoff valve?

In most houses it is where the water line enters the building, in a basement, crawl space, garage wall or utility closet. There may also be a valve at the meter near the street. Find it before an emergency and make sure the handle turns.

Will homeowner insurance cover a burst pipe?

Many policies cover sudden bursts and the resulting damage, but not the pipe itself or slow leaks from wear. Take photographs and call your insurer. Each policy is different.

Should I repair or replace a pipe after it bursts?

A single burst on a healthy line can be repaired. If the pipe is old, corroded or has failed before, replacement of the run is usually the better value.
