Glen Royall Mill Village

Plumber in Glen Royall Mill Village, Wake Forest, NC

Glen Royall Mill Village sits just north of downtown Wake Forest and holds some of the oldest working-family housing in town. This page explains what the village's history suggests about its plumbing, which parts are local fact and which are general advice, and who to call for the water and permits side of a job.

We are not claiming to know the pipe inside any particular house. What we can do is plan the visit around the age of the building and look first at the parts that age tends to affect.

Local note

What the public record says about the village

The Town of Wake Forest's historic district page describes Glen Royall as one of the most intact mill villages in Wake County, with a period of significance from 1900 to 1949. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on August 27, 1999. The mill closed in April 1976.

The National Register listing says the village housing was built by a contractor from plans prepared by the mill superintendent. The dominant house type is a four-room cottage with a pyramidal roof and a central brick flue. Triple-A cottages and shotgun houses make up most of the rest. The main north-south streets are Elizabeth Street and Mill Street.

The same record says house lots were not surveyed until 1941, when the mill decided to sell the houses. The village was incorporated as the Town of Royall Cotton Mills in 1907, lost its charter in 1945 and was annexed into Wake Forest in 1977.

What that age can mean for the plumbing

Houses planned in the early 1900s were small, and the record describes four-room cottages as the main type. General plumbing practice of that time did not assume an indoor bathroom in every house. Where a bathroom, kitchen sink or water heater was added later, the supply and drain lines were often added around the original walls. We do not know which houses in the village were changed this way, so we look rather than assume.

Pipe of that period was often iron, clay or lead-containing material. A house that has never been repiped may still have some of it. Mixed materials in one house are common, and each joint between them is a place worth checking.

Signs an older house needs a closer look

These are general signs and apply to any house of this age, not only the village.

Low pressure at one fixture

A line narrowed by years of mineral buildup restricts flow even when the rest of the house runs fine.

Brown or rusty water after sitting

Corroded iron supply pipe can tint water that has stood overnight. See repiping for the options.

Slow drains in more than one place

Several slow drains point to the main line. A sewer camera inspection shows what is inside it.

Mature trees near the line

The record notes the village's shade tree patterns are preserved. Roots can enter older clay joints, a general risk we check for.

How we approach a repair in an older house

A cable machine clears a blocked drain quickly, and drain cleaning is the usual first step. If the line backs up again, we recommend a camera so you can see the cause rather than guess at it. When the camera shows a broken or root-filled section, sewer line repair is the lasting fix.

For supply lines, a leak inside a wall is hard to locate by eye. Leak detection narrows it down before anyone opens drywall or plaster, which protects the finishes that give an older house its character.

Who handles water, sewer and permits

Raleigh Water runs and bills the water and sewer system for Wake Forest. Its Customer Care line is 919-996-3245, and the Town's website says the city repairs the main and the connection to the meter. Anything between the meter and the house, or inside it, is the homeowner's responsibility.

The Town of Wake Forest Inspections Department at 301 S. Brooks St. handles permits for plumbing work inside the Town, and its number is 919-435-9531. Because the village is a National Register district, ask the Town's Planning staff before changing anything visible from the street. The Town lists 919-435-9516 for historic district questions.

Glen Royall plumbing questions

Do you work on houses in Glen Royall Mill Village?

Yes. Call (919) 944-7281 with your street address and a short description of the problem, and the dispatcher will schedule a plumber. Tell them the house is in the mill village, so the visit can be planned around an older building.

Will a plumber replace original pipe without harming a historic house?

A good plumber chooses access points that limit damage to plaster and trim and uses detection first so fewer walls are opened. Where a repipe is needed, the plan should say where holes will go before any work starts.

Do I need a permit for plumbing work in the village?

Replacing or extending supply, waste or vent lines normally needs a permit from the Town of Wake Forest Inspections Department at 919-435-9531. Minor fixture repairs often do not. Ask when you book and we will tell you which side of that line your job falls on.

Who do I call if the street main is leaking?

A leak in the main is Raleigh Water's responsibility, and its number is 919-996-3245. A leak between the meter and your house is a plumbing call to us.
