Wake Forest neighborhoods and landmarks we serve

Wake Forest began as a college town in 1834 and was incorporated in 1880. The historic core still holds the Southeastern Baptist Theological Seminary, which occupies the original Wake Forest College campus, and the Wake Forest Historical Museum in the Calvin Jones House, built around 1820 on North Main Street. Houses near the core often date from the early 1900s, so older supply and drain lines are common there.

The Glen Royall Mill Village is one of the most intact mill villages in Wake County and dates from 1900 to 1949. The Downtown Wake Forest Historic District covers brick buildings from about 1890 to 1949. For a house in either area, our sewer camera inspection and repiping pages cover what age tends to bring.

Newer housing fills the town along Capital Blvd, NC 98 and Forestville Road, and the Wake Tech North Campus sits on the Louisburg Road corridor. Heritage High School and Wake Forest High School serve the area. Wherever your house sits, the same dispatch line reaches a licensed plumber.

Rolesville and the towns around us

Our dedicated page covers Rolesville and the 27571 ZIP code, with its own notes on utilities and permits. Rolesville is the first town east of Wake Forest and shares the same Raleigh Water service.

Dispatch plans by address and traffic, not by town line. A call from the Capital Blvd side of Wake Forest is routed differently from one near Rolesville, and the crew works out the route when it takes the job. That is why we ask for a landmark as well as an address.

We also take calls from North Raleigh, Wakefield, Wendell, Knightdale, Zebulon, Youngsville and Franklinton. Those towns do not have their own pages, so tell the dispatcher your address and they will confirm coverage and give you a straight answer.

Who handles what in Wake Forest

Raleigh Water operates the water and sewer system that serves Wake Forest and bills the accounts. The Town's website says the city repairs damage in the main and between the street main and the meter, and that the customer is responsible for pipes that break between the meter and the home or inside it.

The Town of Wake Forest runs its own Inspections Department at 301 S. Brooks St. It provides building inspections and permitting inside the Town's jurisdiction. A plumbing permit question for a water heater or a repipe should go to that office at 919-435-9531.

Flood-prone ground and what it means for plumbing

The Town's Tropical Storm Idalia notice in 2023 named flood-prone spots including Durham Road near Richland Creek and Forestville Road near Toms Creek. If you live near a creek or a low road, our sump pump and basement waterproofing pages explain how drainage works. The Town's Floodplain Manager can be reached at 919-435-9445 for a flood zone question.

Water quality across the area

Raleigh Water's 2025 drinking water report lists the water it supplies as soft, at about 1.44 to 1.49 grains per gallon, with a pH near 8.4 and chloramine as the disinfectant. That applies in Wake Forest and Rolesville alike. Soft water leaves little scale in heaters and fixtures, which is one reason we look at age first on a failing unit. For a taste or odor question, the city's drinking water laboratory is at 919-996-4420.

What to have ready when you call

Have your street address, the nearest landmark and a short description of what you see. Say where the water is coming from, whether you have shut off the main valve and whether anyone is at risk. If the property is a business, say so. If you rent, tell the dispatcher so they can plan for a landlord's approval.
