Traditions is a large planned community on the west side of the Wake Forest Reservoir, east of downtown. This page explains what is known about the area, the plumbing problems that newer houses tend to have and who to call for utilities and permits.

What is on the record about Traditions

A local report on Wake Forest subdivisions describes Traditions as a roughly 900-acre mixed-use project on the west side of the reservoir, reaching into Franklin County. It says the land was sold by the Ammons family in 2011 to a new owner, with a separate firm building the infrastructure.

The Town's Dunn Creek Greenway page mentions the greenway network built with Traditions, and it names the Orchards at Traditions, Flaherty Farms and a Del Webb community as places the planned trail connects. The reservoir itself is a Town park, built in the early 1960s as a drinking water source and returned to the Town in September 2019.

A note on town lines

The record says Traditions extends into Franklin County. A few streets may fall under a different permit office than the Town of Wake Forest. Tell the dispatcher your address, and check with the Town or the county before any permit is applied for.

Typical plumbing issues in newer houses

The land sale dates to 2011, so houses in the community are relatively recent builds. General experience with newer homes is that the first problems are often a builder-grade water heater, a toilet that runs, a faucet cartridge that leaks or a garbage disposal that jams. None of these are specific to any one address.

Newer homes also carry more built-in equipment, such as tankless heaters, whole-house shutoffs and irrigation. Each adds a place where something can fail, and a high water bill with no visible leak often points to an irrigation or toilet leak.

Water, sewer and who to call

Raleigh Water serves Wake Forest, and its customer line is 919-996-3245. A leak in the street or in the main is its responsibility, and a leak on your side of the meter is a plumbing call to us. If you are not sure which side the problem is on, call and we will help you work it out.

For plumbing permits in the Town of Wake Forest, the Inspections Department is at 301 S. Brooks St., 919-435-9531.

Planning a project before it becomes an emergency

A newer house gives you a chance to act early. Know where the main shutoff is, and test it once a year so it turns when you need it. Check the water heater's age on its label, and look at the base of the tank for rust or damp. These are general habits and apply to any house, not only the ones in this community.

If you plan a larger change, such as a new fixture or a heater swap, ask about the permit when you book. A plumber who explains the permit step up front saves you a delay on the day of the work. See plumbing installation for what a planned job involves.

Living near the reservoir

The Town's reservoir page describes a 1.6-mile soft trail, a boat launch and a parking lot, and a park open from dawn to dusk. Many homes in the area look alike, so give the dispatcher a landmark such as the reservoir parking lot or the nearest greenway access when you call.

Traditions plumbing questions

Do you serve homes in Traditions?

Yes. Call (919) 944-7281 and give your street address, and the dispatcher will schedule a plumber. Mention the nearest landmark if your street is new and not yet on every map.

Which permit office covers my house?

It depends on where your address sits. Most of the community is in the Town of Wake Forest, and the Inspections Department handles permits there at 919-435-9531. A part of the project reaches into Franklin County, so check your address with the Town first.

My water bill jumped but I see no leak. What now?

A running toilet or an irrigation line is a common hidden source. A meter test with the house shut off shows whether water is moving. Our leak detection service can trace the cause.

Is the water heater my responsibility?

Inside the house, yes. Warranty terms vary by builder and by manufacturer, so check yours before booking a repair or a replacement.
