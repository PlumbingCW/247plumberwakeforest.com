Historic Wake Forest is the oldest part of town, centered on North Main Street and the original college campus. This page covers what the Town's records say about the area, what age can mean for a house's plumbing and the offices to call before you start a project.

What the Town's records say

The Town of Wake Forest lists one local historic district and three National Register districts. The local district, designated on May 10, 1979, is described as the town's first. It follows North Main Street, formerly called Faculty Avenue, from North Avenue to Oak Avenue, and includes North Avenue and East South Avenue.

The Wake Forest Historic District was added to the National Register on December 18, 2003. The Town describes a period of significance from about 1820 to 1953, and says the district holds the original college campus and nearby homes of faculty, staff and students. The Downtown district, listed in 2002, is described as the commercial core with buildings from 1890 to 1949.

Local note

Two different kinds of property

The records separate the downtown commercial core from the surrounding historic district of houses and campus buildings. A brick storefront and a faculty house from the same century can have very different plumbing needs. Residential work on a house is covered on this page, and business work is covered under commercial plumbing.

Plumbing in houses that span a century

A house from the 1800s or early 1900s has usually been updated more than once. A kitchen from the 1950s, a bathroom added in the 1970s and a water heater from this decade can all exist in one building. Each addition may use a different pipe material, and the joints between them are a common place for leaks. This is general to older housing and is not a statement about any one address.

Because we cannot know the history of your house in advance, we start by looking. A plumber who traces which pipe feeds which fixture can give a clear price for a targeted fix and may avoid a larger job.

What this means for your repair

Each period of building left its own pipe behind, so the practical step is to identify the material before pricing the work. A repair that replaces a section of supply or drain line is done to the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, and the Town's Inspections Department at 919-435-9531 can say whether the job needs a permit and an inspection.

Before the visit, shut off the main valve if water is escaping, photograph any stain and note when it appears. That short record helps a plumber narrow the cause and keeps the price clear before work starts.

Before you start a project in the historic district

The Town's historic district pages say properties in the National Register districts may qualify for preservation tax credits. They also say the National Register gives protection from adverse effects of projects that use federal or state money. The local historic district has its own rules.

We do not interpret those rules for you. Ask the Town's Planning staff before changing anything visible from the street, such as a vent, a meter enclosure or an exterior fixture. The Town lists 919-435-9516 for historic district questions, and 919-435-9531 for the Inspections Department that handles plumbing permits.

Water, sewer and drainage

Raleigh Water supplies and bills water and sewer service in Wake Forest, at 919-996-3245. The city handles the main and the connection to the meter. Everything from the meter to the house belongs to the homeowner. Older streets with mature trees can be hard on drain lines, and a sewer camera inspection is the plain way to see the condition of yours.

Historic Wake Forest plumbing questions

Do you work on houses near North Main Street?

Yes. Call (919) 944-7281 and give your street address. Tell the dispatcher the house is in the historic core so the plumber arrives expecting an older building.

Can you tell how old my pipes are?

A plumber can often judge the material and age by sight at accessible points, and a camera shows drain lines. We will tell you what we find and what we cannot see without opening a wall.

Do I need approval before plumbing work in the historic district?

Interior plumbing normally needs a permit from the Town of Wake Forest Inspections Department when supply, waste or vent lines change. Changes that show from the street may need extra review, so ask Town Planning first.

Is this page for businesses downtown too?

This page is about houses. Downtown businesses can use our commercial plumbing service, and the same number reaches the dispatcher.
