After-hours calls for businesses

Commercial failures tend to happen at the busiest times. A restaurant drain backs up in the middle of a dinner service, a roof-level leak drips through a ceiling after closing, and a shared water heater quits on a Monday. Dispatch handles these on the same line as home calls, and you tell the dispatcher it is a business so the right crew is booked.

Tell them the building type, the number of tenants affected and whether the problem is in your suite or in a shared line. Commercial buildings often have shared mains, and the responsible party depends on your lease.

Who owns the pipe: tenant or landlord?

A tenant normally controls fixtures inside the suite. The landlord usually owns shared mains, risers and the building's service line. The lease decides, and a plumber cannot settle that, but we can tell you where the failed part sits so you know whom to call.

If the failed line is shared, we document the problem with photographs and a written description so the property manager can approve work quickly.

Kitchen drains, grease and hydro jetting

Kitchen lines carry the heaviest load in a commercial building. Raleigh Water runs a fats, oils and grease program, and it states that grease sticks to pipes and builds up, which can cause sewer backups and overflows. A restaurant on Capital Blvd that skips regular line cleaning will see a backup sooner or later.

Scheduled hydro jetting clears the wall of the line before it fails. Small interior grease traps need cleaning on a schedule, and we ask for details of yours on the call.

Backflow, water heaters and restrooms

Many commercial buildings have backflow preventers that protect the public water supply from contamination. Ask Raleigh Water or the Town of Wake Forest Inspections Department at 919-435-9531 which devices on your property need testing and how often, since the rule depends on your building and use.

Commercial water heaters run harder than a home unit, and a failure in a small restaurant or salon can stop service. A planned swap through water heater installation beats an emergency one. Restroom fixtures, flush valves and sensor faucets round out the routine calls.

Commercial leak detection: an honest note

A leak in a commercial building can sit in a slab, a shared wall or a roof drain, and finding it can take more than one visit. We say up front when a leak may be outside our scope or the owner's responsibility, because a clear answer beats a long job that ends in a dispute. Our leak detection page describes the methods.
