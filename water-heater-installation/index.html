Planning the project

Four decisions shape a new installation. The first is the fuel, gas or electric. The second is capacity, which depends on the number of people and bathrooms. The third is location, since moving a heater means new gas, water and venting runs. The fourth is type, whether a standard tank, hybrid heat pump or tankless unit.

Each decision affects the others. A heat pump heater needs air space, a tankless unit needs a gas line or circuit, and a move across the house means more pipe. A plumber looks at the site before quoting.

What a correct installation includes

A code installation is more than connecting two pipes. A temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe protects against overpressure. A drain pan with a drain line protects the floor in attics and upper floors. A thermal expansion tank is added where a backflow device or pressure reducing valve makes the system closed. Venting for gas units has to be sized and sloped properly.

Gas units get a leak test on every connection and a check of combustion air. Electric units get a check of the breaker size and wiring. The plumber shows you where the shutoffs are before leaving.

Permits and the Wake Forest Inspections Department

Wake Forest has its own Inspections Department at 301 S. Brooks St., phone 919-435-9531. The Town's pages did not state when a plumbing permit is needed for a water heater, so call before you start, particularly for a change in fuel, location or venting.

For towns Wake County permits for, the County's guidance says a like-for-like replacement in a one or two family home is exempt if the energy rate is not greater than the original, the fuel, location and venting do not change and a licensed plumbing contractor does the work. A new installation or a move is not a like-for-like swap and goes through a permit. The County charges $75 for a residential trade permit, which covers plumbing, electrical and mechanical.

Soft water and what it means for your next heater

Raleigh Water's 2025 report lists the water as soft, about 1.44 to 1.49 grains per gallon, with a pH near 8.4. Soft water drops less scale in a tank. That does not remove the need to check the anode rod every few years, and it is the cheapest way to extend the life of a tank water heater.

The code a heater installation follows

Plumbers in North Carolina work to the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, which covers items such as relief valves, discharge piping and venting clearances. Wake County's permit guidance treats a like-for-like heater swap as exempt from a plumbing permit only when fuel, location, venting and piping stay the same and a licensed contractor does the work.

Questions about water heater installation in Wake Forest

How far in advance should I book a water heater installation?

Call as soon as you decide. A booked slot lets you choose the unit and avoid an emergency swap. Many installs happen within days, depending on the schedule.

Is a permit required for a new water heater?

A new installation, a move or a change in fuel is not a like-for-like swap, and permits apply. Call the Town of Wake Forest Inspections Department at 919-435-9531 to confirm.

Should I replace the old tank before it fails?

If the tank is 10 or more years old, replacing it on your schedule is often cheaper than an emergency. Look for rust at the fittings, water around the base and rumbling noises.
